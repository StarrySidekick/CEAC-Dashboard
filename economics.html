The grid serving the metro New York area relies 99% on fossil fuel combustion, and 98% of that is natural gas. When gas prices move, your supply charge moves with them.

The reliability warning behind the rates

In April 2026 the New York Independent System Operator issued its annual Summer Reliability Report. If the state sees a heatwave averaging 95°F for three or more days, the forecast capacity margin is:

−1,679 MW

A negative margin means New York would not have enough electricity on those days, raising the prospect of rolling brownouts. Source: NYISO Short-Term Assessment of Reliability, 2026 Q1.

The solar side

Four ways local solar lowers bills

Community solar projects benefit Westchester in ways that reach well past the subscriber.

1

Free grid infrastructure

When a developer applies for interconnection, the utility specifies the transformers and connection equipment the developer must buy and install, which the utility then owns. Every project strengthens the local grid at no cost to ratepayers.

2

Less imported power

Electricity injected locally reduces the need to import power from far-away upstate sources, which saves the utility on supply costs.

3

A direct subscriber discount

The utility compensates the developer for injected electricity, and the developer must pass a discount to enrolled subscribers, typically 5% to 10% savings.

4

Lease income for site owners

Projects pay the site owner a lease for 20 to 25 years, income the owner would not otherwise earn from that roof or ground space.

Behind-the-meter solar is prepaid electricity

Installing solar or storage on your own property for your own consumption is effectively prepaying for 25 years of electricity. Once you recover the initial investment, the remaining years are free. Even a system covering only half your annual usage has cut your exposure to ever-escalating utility rates by half.

The storage side

Three ways BESS lowers bills

Storage stabilizes the Con Edison grid by shaving peak demand, deferring expensive upgrades, and making renewables usable around the clock.

1

Peak shaving

Systems charge overnight or midday when demand is low, then discharge in the late afternoon and evening when demand peaks. Shifting that load protects transformer substations from overloading and avoids local outages during extreme heat.

2

Deferring costly upgrades

Storage is a non-wires solution that removes the immediate need to build multi-million dollar substations, and relieves congestion where adding cable in dense underground conduit is physically very difficult.

3

Integrating clean energy

Storage harvests solar generated during the day and releases it when production drops, providing a reliable reserve that reduces reliance on older, high-polluting fossil fuel peaker plants sited in local neighborhoods.

Utilities also pay customers directly for this flexibility. Con Edison demand-response programs such as Smart Usage and Grid Rewards™ pay customers to shed load during peak events and deliver those reductions reliably across the year.
